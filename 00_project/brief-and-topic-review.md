## The chosen proposal, as she wrote it

**Idea:** an interactive museum that makes astronomy something visitors experience rather than read. Almost everything we know about the sky reached us as light, and shadows are how we measure it (sundials, eclipses, moon phases, sunspots, the dark between stars).

**Why here:**
- The existing observatory museum is mainly pictorial, with a few models, old instruments, a live solar image and the Fraunhofer spectrum.
- Access to the real observatory is restrictive; night viewing needs permission and clear skies.
- It supports science education: light and shadow are the entry point to optics, orbits, eclipses and seasons.

**Concept, Chiaroscuro:** strong contrast between light and dark gives form, depth and drama. Visitors move from bright, sunlit galleries about the Sun, through dimmer filtered spaces for the Moon and planets, into a dark chamber for stars and deep space. Contrast itself guides the experience.

**Programme listed:** Sun Gallery; tactile interactive exhibits; hands-on workshop; Spectrum Chamber (mirror-room-like, with crystal glass); event space (clearable for telescope night-watches); cafe/canteen; dome theatre; cosmic scale corridor; souvenir shop.

**Target audience:** tourists and hotel guests, school groups, students and researchers.

**Plan:** a ground floor drawn at 1:300 on a regular column grid, about 66 m x 66 m overall, with curved and angled outer walls, toilets and service cores, and several entries.

## Observations to act on (from this research)

1. **Weighting:** interiors ~60%, services and innovation ~25%, site ~15% (family's guidance). Interior depth will come from the light sequence, the exhibit/furniture design, materials and detailing (see `01_research/design/`).
2. **Case-study list needs cleaning.** On the astronomy sheet, "Under, Norway" and "The Blue Planet, Denmark" are aquarium projects carried over from the ocean sheet, and "The Color Inside" (Turrell skyspace, Austin) is relevant but should be framed as a precedent. Replace the aquariums with astronomy/planetarium projects; see `01_research/case-studies/case-studies.md`.
3. **"Cameras are not permitted" and "night viewing needs permission"** should be backed by evidence (signage photo or KSO page) at the site review. Official IIA pages confirm group visits need a form 15 days ahead and a guided 90-minute limit.
4. **Height limit** in Kodaikanal (reported as 10 m / three floors) affects the dome theatre: plan it partly sunk into the slope.
5. **Kodaikanal climate** (cool, very humid Sep-Nov, foggy) affects material choice and makes the indoor sky experiences more valuable.
6. **Keep the tech light:** the strongest exhibits here are physical and optical (pinhole rooms, sun-disc floor calendar, prisms, orreries, tactile Moon), which also suits the "not tech-heavy" brief.
