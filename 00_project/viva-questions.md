# Likely jury and viva questions, with answer outlines

Status: Draft, 27 Nov 2026. Practise these aloud. The answers are outlines to put in her own words, not scripts.

## Concept

**1. What is Chiaroscuro, and how is it more than a word on your sheet?**
Chiaroscuro is the use of strong contrast between light and dark. Here it becomes a measurable rule: the light ladder sets target lux levels for each zone (about 300-500 lux in the Sun Gallery down to near zero in the star chamber), and light locks between zones give the eyes time to adapt. The route itself is the concept.

**2. Why go from light to dark, and not the other way?**
It follows the eye: going from bright to dark slowly lets the eyes adapt so the dark rooms reveal more and more stars. Going suddenly from dark into bright would dazzle. It also follows the story of astronomy: we start with the Sun, which we can see, and end with deep space, which we only see because the sky is dark.

**3. How long do eyes take to adapt?**
Several minutes for a useful level and up to about an hour for full dark adaptation. So the route steps down in stages, with benches and light locks, rather than one sudden drop.

## Site

**4. Why Kodaikanal?**
It has the Kodaikanal Solar Observatory, founded in 1899, with over 100 years of daily solar records and the 1909 discovery of the Evershed effect, but its museum is mainly posters. The town has millions of tourists a year and school trips. Clear, high-altitude skies suit sky-watching.

**5. How do you deal with the height limit?**
The reported limit is 10 m or three floors. The dome and the dark rooms are sunk into the slope, which also helps darkness, sound and temperature stability.

**6. How does the climate affect the interiors?**
Cool and very humid (87-89% from June to November, about 1,580 mm of rain). So: moisture-resistant boards (BWP ply, HDHMR), sealed finishes, dehumidification, mild heating instead of cooling, covered entrances.

## Programme and planning

**7. Who is it for?**
Tourists and hotel guests, school groups, students and researchers. The plan has a separate school entry, group rotation between dome, workshop and galleries, and a bright public zone (cafe, shop) outside the dark sequence.

**8. Why a 10-12 m dome and not a big one like Birla?**
Birla Chennai is 15 m for a city; Kodaikanal is a small town. A 10-12 m dome with 60-90 seats matches one or two school groups, costs less, and can double as the event and talk space.

**9. What if someone feels uncomfortable in the dark?**
There is a short-cut exit at the midpoint, lit handrails and floor strips, benches, staff on hand, and every dark room has a lit exit.

## Services and safety

**10. What happens in a fire during a dome show?**
The fire alarm automatically stops the show and raises house lights to full; exit signs are always lit; emergency lights run on emergency power; exits are separate from the light locks; materials are non-combustible or fire-retardant.

**11. How do you keep light from leaking between rooms?**
Light locks with turns, baffle walls or double doors; dark, matt finishes near openings; no glass between dark rooms.

**12. How do you deal with sound in the dome?**
A hemisphere focuses sound, so the dome is perforated with absorbent backing. The Sabine calculation gives the absorption needed for a reverberation time of about 0.6 s.

**13. How is the building accessible?**
Ramps and lifts per the Harmonised Guidelines, 900 mm minimum doors, 1,500 mm turning spaces, wheelchair seats in the dome, tactile paths and Braille labels, a tactile Moon model, and counters with an 800 mm-high section.

## Interiors and exhibits

**14. What is interactive, if it is not tech-heavy?**
Mostly physical and analogue: the sun-disc floor calendar, live Sun projection, a prism room, a phases ring, a tactile Moon, a hand-cranked orrery, a scale corridor, and a switch that "turns on the city" to show light pollution.

**15. Why these materials?**
They follow the light: warm stone and timber in the Sun Gallery, silver-grey and soft in the Moon gallery, matt black, felt and fabric in the dark rooms (they absorb light and sound). All are chosen for humidity and fire performance.

**16. What is your innovation?**
Designing a building around darkness and the human eye, and making that safe and comfortable; using the building itself as an instrument (oculus, Zero Shadow Day marker); and protecting the observatory's night sky with dark-sky lighting.

## Case studies

**17. What did you take from Shanghai Astronomy Museum?**
Architecture as an instrument: the Oculus, the Inverted Dome and the Sphere track the Sun and sky. The Sun Gallery's oculus and floor calendar come from this idea at a smaller scale.

**18. What did you learn from Birla Planetarium?**
Practical dome numbers (15 m, 10° tilt, hybrid projection), crowd handling for school groups, and keeping the old projector as an exhibit: heritage on display.

## Quick numbers to remember

- KSO founded 1899; Evershed effect 1909
- Altitude about 2,133-2,343 m; latitude about 10.2° N
- Humidity 87-89% June-November; rain about 1,580 mm
- Height cap reported 10 m / three floors
- Area about 2,500 m² net, 3,100 m² gross
- Dome 10-12 m, 60-90 seats
- Light ladder: about 300-500 lux down to near 0
- Zero Shadow Days around mid-April and late August
