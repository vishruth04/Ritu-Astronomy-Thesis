# Thesis report outline

Status: Draft, 27 Nov 2026. A chapter plan for the written report, based on the usual structure of Indian B.Des interior design theses. Adjust it to the official Sathyabama format when the guide shares it (page size, font, margins, binding and word count are not yet known).

## Front matter

1. Title page (project title, name, register number, course S102BPROJ, department, institution, year)
2. Bonafide certificate (college format)
3. Declaration
4. Acknowledgements
5. Abstract (200-300 words: problem, site, concept, outcome)
6. Table of contents, list of figures, list of tables, abbreviations

## Chapter 1: Introduction

- 1.1 Background: astronomy, public science and museums in India
- 1.2 Need for the project: KSO's 125-year heritage shown mainly through posters; tourists and school groups in Kodaikanal with little hands-on science
- 1.3 Aim (one sentence) and objectives (4-6 bullet points)
- 1.4 Scope and limitations (interiors-led; site and structure at concept level)
- 1.5 Methodology (diagram: literature → case studies → site → programme → concept → design → services → details)

## Chapter 2: Literature study

- 2.1 Astronomy basics relevant to the exhibits (use the astronomy primer)
- 2.2 Light, darkness and human vision: dark adaptation, lux levels, glare
- 2.3 Museum and exhibition design principles: circulation, interactivity, labels
- 2.4 Planetarium design: dome sizes, seating, projection, acoustics
- 2.5 Standards: NBC 2016 Part 4, Harmonised Guidelines for accessibility
- 2.6 Chiaroscuro in art and architecture (Caravaggio, Rembrandt; Turrell, Tadao Ando's Church of the Light, K)

## Chapter 3: Case studies

- 3.1 Method and criteria
- 3.2 International cases (3)
- 3.3 National cases (2-3)
- 3.4 Live case(s) with own measurements and photos
- 3.5 Comparative analysis table
- 3.6 Inferences for the design

## Chapter 4: Site study

- 4.1 Kodaikanal: location, history, tourism
- 4.2 The Kodaikanal Solar Observatory
- 4.3 Site: location, area, contours, access, surroundings
- 4.4 Climate analysis and sun path
- 4.5 Bylaws and planning rules
- 4.6 SWOT and site inferences

## Chapter 5: User study and programme

- 5.1 User groups and their needs
- 5.2 Visitor numbers and group sizes (assumptions stated)
- 5.3 Area programme
- 5.4 Adjacency and zoning

## Chapter 6: Design concept

- 6.1 Chiaroscuro: from sunlight into the dark
- 6.2 The light ladder (lux targets) and light locks
- 6.3 Visitor journey and storyboard
- 6.4 Form and section strategy (sinking dark spaces into the slope)

## Chapter 7: Design development

- 7.1 Plans and zoning
- 7.2 Space-by-space design (use the room-by-room guide)
- 7.3 Exhibits and interactivity
- 7.4 Materials and finishes
- 7.5 Furniture and joinery

## Chapter 8: Services and innovation

- 8.1 Lighting design and control
- 8.2 Fire and life safety in dark spaces
- 8.3 HVAC and humidity
- 8.4 Acoustics (with the dome RT calculation)
- 8.5 Accessibility
- 8.6 Dark-sky exterior lighting
- 8.7 What is innovative

## Chapter 9: Conclusion

- What the project achieves, what could be developed further.

## Back matter

- References (one consistent style, for example APA or Harvard; copy from the sources list)
- Appendices: drawings list, area statement, survey/questionnaire if done, live-visit notes, photographs

## Writing tips

- Each chapter ends with "Inferences" in 3-5 bullets that lead to design decisions.
- Every figure gets a number, a caption and a source.
- Keep text short and let diagrams carry the argument; this is a design report.
