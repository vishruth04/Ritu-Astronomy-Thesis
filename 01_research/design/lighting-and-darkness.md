# Lighting and darkness: making Chiaroscuro work

Status: Draft research, 27 Nov 2026. Tags: A primary, B secondary, C aggregator, K general practice (verify).

The whole concept is a sequence from bright to dark. Lighting is therefore the main interior design tool, not a service added at the end. This is where most of the "interior scope" the reviewers asked for will come from.

## 1. How eyes adapt (the science behind the route)

- Adapting from bright to dark "does take several minutes", and final dark adaptation "can take up to an hour". The Canadian Conservation Institute recommends designing "a transition into exhibit spaces so that visitors can adapt in stages". Reduce foyer brightness, because a dark gallery seen from a bright foyer looks closed. ([CCI, Light](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/agents-deterioration/light.html), A)
- Adaptation mechanisms range from 200 milliseconds to an hour. ([Innes, "Why is it so dark in here?"](https://www.napier.ac.uk/-/media/worktribe/output-196477/whyisitsodarkinhere2011librepdf.ashx), B)
- Older eyes need much more light: a 45-year-old may need about 300 lux to see what a 25-year-old sees at 50 lux. ([Innes](https://www.napier.ac.uk/-/media/worktribe/output-196477/whyisitsodarkinhere2011librepdf.ashx), B; [ERCO](https://erco.com/en/designing-with-light/mediaassetpool/lighting-technology/museum-lighting-with-50lx-6481), B)
- Rod vision (true night vision) is insensitive to deep red light, which is why observatories use red lights. (K)

**Design rule that follows:** step the light down gradually along the route, never jump from bright to black. Each step is a threshold space (a "light lock") that doubles as a story beat: Sun → twilight → Moon → night → deep space.

## 2. Suggested light ladder for the route (K, for her to refine)

| Zone (her programme) | Mood | Target general light | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry / ticketing / shop | Bright, welcoming | 200-300 lux | Daylight, warm; shop is outside the dark sequence |
| Sun Gallery | Brightest, daylit | Daylight + 300-500 lux on exhibits | Heliostat/coelostat-style daylight beam, sundial floor, live solar projection (white-light projection onto a screen, never direct viewing) |
| Spectrum Chamber | Coloured, filtered | Controlled, 50-150 lux | Prism / dichroic glass splits a single light source into the spectrum. Link to the Fraunhofer spectrum at KSO |
| Moon and planets | Dim, silver | 50-100 lux general, accents on objects | Raking light on lunar relief models shows phases and craters (chiaroscuro at its most literal) |
| Cosmic scale corridor | Transitional | 10-50 lux, decreasing | Light lock before the dark zone; floor-level wayfinding light |
| Stars / deep space chamber | Dark | Near 0 lux ambient; points of light only | Fibre-optic star field, red step lights, glow-edge handrails |
| Dome theatre | Dark | 0 during show; cove light for entry | Separate light-locked entry and exit |
| Workshop / event space / cafe | Normal task light | 300-500 lux | Outside the sequence, so groups can rest; event space must go fully dark for telescope nights |

## 3. Conservation and comfort numbers (A/B)

- 50 lux: benchmark for light-sensitive objects (paper, textiles, photographs). 150 lux: paintings, wood. 300 lux (CCI) to about 500 lux (Getty): stone and metal. ([CCI](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/agents-deterioration/light.html); [Getty](https://www.getty.edu/news/why-is-it-so-dark-in-the-galleries))
- Visibility multipliers of up to 3x each for dark surfaces, fine detail and older viewers. (CCI)
- UV: daylight through glass about 400-500 µW/lm; good filter about 75 µW/lm; white LED 0-75. (CCI summary)
- Colour temperature: warm 2,800-3,000 K at low levels; cooler only at high levels. CRI 85 minimum, 90+ preferred. Warm light at 50 lux looks dimmer than neutral light at the same level. (CCI; Innes)
- Glare: snoots, baffles and louvres on spotlights; move reflections below eye level; mock up reflections with plastic film; dark matte backgrounds make objects read better than glossy bright ones. (CCI)

Her exhibits are mostly interactive models, not fragile originals (except anything lent by KSO, such as old instruments or photographic plates, which must follow the 50 lux rule).

## 4. Daylight as an exhibit (the Sun end)

Ideas to develop (K):
- **Oculus or light slot** that casts a moving disc of sunlight across a floor calendar marked with solstices, equinoxes and local noon (Kodaikanal is at about 10.2° N, so the noon sun passes nearly overhead in late April and mid August). Precedent: Ennead's Oculus at the Shanghai Astronomy Museum lands on a circular platform at summer-solstice noon.
- **Meridian line** in the floor (like historic cathedral meridians).
- **Coelostat-style mirror** feeding a projected image of the Sun into the gallery, echoing KSO's own coelostat. Safe, and live when the sky is clear.
- **Pinhole camera room** (camera obscura) that projects the sky or the landscape. Cheap, strong, very "chiaroscuro".
- On cloudy days these become dark, which is honest; pair each with a fallback (projected archive images from the KSO plates).

## 5. Darkness as a material (the Stars end)

- Matte black or very dark blue finishes with low reflectance (paint, acoustic felt, stained timber). Avoid glossy black: it reflects every light.
- Fibre-optic star fields: one remote light source feeds hundreds of fibres, so the ceiling has no heat or electronics, and maintenance is in an accessible box (see `materials-and-finishes.md`).
- Safety in the dark: low-level step lights, glow-in-the-dark or LED nosings, handrail lights, emergency lighting that is normally hidden but meets code (see `../standards/codes-and-accessibility.md`).
- Sound becomes more important as light drops: soft floors and absorptive walls make dark rooms calm.

## 6. Exterior lighting and the dark sky

The museum sits beside a working observatory. Shielded, downward, warm (2,700 K or lower, amber where possible) exterior lighting, curfews after events, and no uplighting. Frame this as "the museum protects the sky it is about". (K; check IDA dark-sky lighting principles.)
