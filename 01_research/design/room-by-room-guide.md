# Room-by-room design guide

Status: Draft, 27 Nov 2026. One sheet of design intent per space, in visitor order. Each entry lists what the room is for, its size and light, how it should feel, what goes in it, what to draw, and the services it needs. Areas come from the area programme; light levels from the light ladder. Items marked (K) are design suggestions from general practice for her to adapt, not fixed facts.

## How to read this guide

- **Light** = the target general light level. Exhibit accents can be higher.
- **Ceiling** = suggested clear height. Keep under the 10 m height limit overall by sinking dark rooms into the slope.
- **Drawings** = what to produce for design development (plan 1:50, RCP, sections, details).

---

## 1. Arrival: forecourt, lobby, tickets, cloak (≈150 m²)

- **Purpose:** welcome, gather a school group of 40-50, buy tickets, leave bags and umbrellas (Kodaikanal is wet for half the year).
- **Light:** 200-300 lux, daylight from the valley side. The brightest "ordinary" space before the Sun Gallery.
- **Feel:** open, warm, timber and stone, a view toward KSO's domes so visitors see the real observatory before they enter.
- **Key elements:** ticket and information counter with an 800 mm-high accessible section and knee space; a covered drop-off and wet-umbrella stand; a large "your journey from light to dark" map in the floor or wall (the concept explained in one image); lockers; a group-gathering zone with steps that double as seating.
- **Drawings:** lobby plan with furniture, counter detail 1:10, wayfinding map design.
- **Services:** air lock or vestibule for cold and rain; heated floor mat or entry grille; CCTV; first-aid room nearby.

## 2. Sun Gallery (≈350 m²) — brightest

- **Purpose:** the Sun, KSO's history, daylight as a living exhibit.
- **Light:** daylight plus 300-500 lux on exhibits. Use controlled daylight: clerestories and one oculus, not glare.
- **Feel:** golden, warm, high and bright; stone floor that glows in the beam.
- **Key elements:**
  - **Oculus and sun-disc floor calendar:** an opening in the roof throws a disc of sunlight on the floor; brass markers show where the disc falls at noon on solstices, equinoxes and the two Zero Shadow Days.
  - **Live Sun projection:** a coelostat (sun-tracking mirror) on the roof sends sunlight down to a white projection table, showing today's real sunspots (as at KSO). Simple, analogue, safe.
  - **KSO story wall:** the 1899 founding, Evershed's 1909 discovery, the 125-year sunspot record shown as a long relief graph visitors can touch.
  - **Solar heat exhibit:** a parabolic dish focuses sunlight onto a safe target (K: supervised).
- **Drawings:** plan, RCP with oculus and clerestory, a section showing the sun angles for 21 Dec, 21 Mar and 21 Jun, a detail of the floor markers.
- **Services:** daylight sensors that dim the electric lights; blinds for cloudy-day projection; UV-filtering glass where artefacts sit.

## 3. Spectrum Chamber (≈80 m²) — colour

- **Purpose:** show that white light is made of colours, and that colours are fingerprints of elements.
- **Light:** 50-150 lux, mostly coloured light from one source.
- **Feel:** a jewel box. A dark, small, intimate room where one white beam explodes into colour.
- **Key elements:** a large glass prism or a column of dichroic glass panels; mirrored or polished black walls to multiply the colours (the "crystal-glass mirror room" from her topic sheet); a slow motorised or hand-turned glass disc; a spectroscope station where visitors look at different lamps (sodium, neon, hydrogen) and see different lines.
- **Drawings:** plan and an unfolded wall elevation showing where the colours land; a detail of the prism mount; a light-path diagram.
- **Services:** a single high-intensity light source (daylight via a heliostat on sunny days, an LED or xenon projector on cloudy days, K); heat and cleaning access to glass.

## 4. Light lock 1: "Dusk"

- A short, turning passage that drops from about 100 to 50 lux. Warm to cool light. One sentence on the wall: "As the Sun sets, the sky's other lights appear."
- Two turns or a baffle wall so no direct light leaks into the next room. Width 1.8 m minimum for two-way wheelchair passing.

## 5. Moon and planets gallery (≈300 m²) — dim, silver

- **Purpose:** phases, craters, eclipses, the planets.
- **Light:** 50-100 lux general, raking accents on models.
- **Feel:** cool, silver-grey, quiet; soft floor.
- **Key elements:**
  - **Phases ring:** one strong lamp (the "Sun") and 8 white spheres on a circle; walk around and watch the phases.
  - **Tactile Moon:** a 1-1.5 m relief Moon, lit from a low angle; Braille label.
  - **Hand-cranked orrery table:** a mechanical model of the planets visitors turn; a child-height and a wheelchair-height version.
  - **Planet weight scales:** "What you would weigh on Mars."
  - **Eclipse window:** a sphere passes in front of a lamp to show a solar eclipse.
- **Drawings:** plan, RCP showing accent lights, orrery table detail 1:10, tactile Moon mounting detail.
- **Services:** track lighting on dimmers; quiet air supply.

## 6. Cosmic scale corridor (≈120 m², about 40 m × 3 m) — dimming

- **Purpose:** feel the emptiness of space; the light lock into the dark zone.
- **Light:** 50 lux at the start dimming to 10 lux at the end; floor-level wayfinding light.
- **Feel:** a long walk where the planets get further apart and the light fades.
- **Key elements:** the Sun as a 11 mm bead at the start; planet markers at 1 AU = 1.2 m (Neptune at 36 m); planet-size models in wall niches; a closing wall: "At this scale the nearest star is 320 km away." A floor strip changes texture at each planet for tactile wayfinding.
- **Drawings:** long plan and section with the light gradient drawn as a tone bar; a niche detail.
- **Services:** dimmable linear lights in the skirting; benches at about 30 m.

## 7. Light lock 2: "Nightfall"

- Drops to about 1-5 lux with red or amber step lights. A bench to wait while the eyes adjust. A short audio cue (crickets, wind) helps the transition.
- Doors or a double baffle so dome and star chamber stay dark.

## 8. Star and deep-space chamber (≈200 m²) — darkest gallery

- **Purpose:** the experience of a truly dark sky; the Milky Way as people rarely see it.
- **Light:** near 0 lux ambient; points of light only.
- **Feel:** awe and stillness. Soft, absorbent surfaces; low benches and reclining seats; sound becomes important.
- **Key elements:**
  - **Fibre-optic star ceiling:** constellations visible from Kodaikanal; colour-coded stars (red cool, blue hot); a soft Milky Way band; slow twinkle mode.
  - **Nakshatra ring:** the 27 lunar mansions in a ring of low light at the edge.
  - **Light-pollution comparison:** one switch "turns on the city", and the stars vanish; switch off, they return. The concept's whole argument in one action.
  - **Reclining benches** set in rings, with lit edges.
- **Drawings:** RCP of the star ceiling (constellation layout to scale), section showing the illuminator box access and the dark-coloured acoustic panels, bench detail.
- **Services:** fibre illuminators in a ventilated, accessible void outside the room; emergency lights on emergency power; fire alarm brings house lights up automatically; quiet low-velocity air.

## 9. Dome theatre and pre-show (≈220 m²) — dark

- **Purpose:** shows, talks, the event space for school groups and night programmes.
- **Size:** 10-12 m dome, 60-90 seats, concentric flat floor (cheaper, flexible, suits talks).
- **Feel:** a planetarium in miniature; cove light glowing at the dome's edge as people enter.
- **Key elements:** perforated aluminium dome; reclining seats; a central or cove-mounted projector; wheelchair spaces (at least 2-3 for 60-90 seats; the norm is 6 up to 600 seats, K for exact counts); separate light-locked entry and exit; a small pre-show foyer with a "what you will see tonight" screen.
- **Drawings:** plan with seat layout and sightlines, section through the dome with the cove and catwalk, cove detail, seat-row detail.
- **Services:** acoustic absorption behind the dome; projection room; AHU with silencers; emergency lighting; house lights linked to the fire alarm.

## 10. Exit, workshop and event space (≈150 m² + 200 m² + terrace)

- **Purpose:** return to light; make and do; telescope nights.
- **Light:** 300-500 lux for work; the event space must go fully dark for observing.
- **Feel:** a bright, practical studio after the dark; terrace facing the open sky.
- **Key elements:** workshop tables for sundial-making, pinhole cameras, simple telescopes and spectroscopes; storage walls; the event space opening onto an **observing terrace** with no upward lights and red-only lighting during sky-watch sessions (KSO runs evening sky-watching 6:30-7:30 pm; partner with them).
- **Drawings:** workshop plan with furniture, storage elevation, terrace plan with telescope pads.
- **Services:** sinks, task lights, power points at table level, separate entry for schools.

## 11. Cafe and souvenir shop (≈180 m² + 60 m²)

- **Purpose:** rest, eat, buy; outside the dark sequence.
- **Light:** normal, daylit.
- **Feel:** valley views, a "Sun" theme in materials (warm timber, brass), menu ideas such as a "Zero Shadow" dessert for fun.
- **Key elements:** outdoor terrace (covered for rain), shop near the exit with KSO-themed products (star maps, sundial kits, sunspot postcards).
- **Services:** kitchen exhaust kept away from the observing terrace and its sky.

## 12. Back of house

- Projection and server room (≈30 m²), exhibit maintenance workshop and store (≈80 m²), admin (≈60 m²), plant (≈80 m²), loading bay, staff toilets.
- Keep service doors out of the dark rooms' sightlines; give every dark room a lit, separate service route for staff.

## Choosing 5-7 spaces to detail

Recommended for the detailing review, because they carry the concept and the interior weighting:

1. Sun-disc floor calendar and oculus (Sun Gallery)
2. Prism / dichroic installation (Spectrum Chamber)
3. Tactile Moon with raking light (Moon gallery)
4. Hand-cranked orrery table (Moon and planets)
5. Fibre-optic star ceiling with access void (Star chamber)
6. Dome cove and seat-row edge (Dome theatre)
7. A light-lock baffle wall with step and handrail lighting
