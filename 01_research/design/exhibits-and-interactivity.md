# Exhibits and light interactivity

Status: Draft ideas bank, 27 Nov 2026. Brief from family: interiors lead; a few lightly interactive exhibits; **not tech-heavy**. Tags: B secondary, K general practice.

## 1. Principles

- **Hands-on and minds-on:** science-centre research stresses that touching is not enough; the exhibit should make the visitor notice, predict and explain. ([National Academies, Surrounded by Science, ch. 4](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/12614/chapter/4), B)
- **Phenomenon first:** Ennead's Shanghai museum lets visitors engage with "real astronomical phenomena" (sunlight, time, orientation) through the building itself, not only exhibits about space. ([Ennead](https://www.ennead.com/work/shanghai-astronomy-museum), B)
- **Physical and optical beats digital** for this brief: cheaper, durable, works without staff, ages well, and every exhibit is an interior element she can design and detail (furniture, joinery, light).
- **Local story:** tie the Sun end to KSO (125 years of daily solar records, sunspots, Evershed effect, coelostat).

## 3. Exhibit furniture guidelines (K)

- Heights: tabletop exhibits 750-850 mm with 680 mm knee space (wheelchair and children); viewing slots at two heights (1,100 and 1,450 mm).
- Graphics: max 50-75 words per label, large type, Tamil and English; Braille and tactile versions at key exhibits.
- Robust materials: solid wood edges, powder-coated steel, compact laminate, toughened glass; no loose small parts.
- Plan maintenance access to every light source and mechanism (removable panels, a service corridor behind dark rooms).
- Daylight exhibits need a cloudy-day fallback (archive projection, lamp mode).

## 4. Choose 5-7 to develop in detail

A thesis should not try to design all of these. Pick a few to detail at 1:10/1:5 with materials and lighting (good candidates: sun-disc floor, prism room, tactile Moon, orrery table, star chamber ceiling, dome entry).
