# Services: lighting control, HVAC, acoustics, fire and AV

Status: Draft, 27 Nov 2026. Services carry about 25% of the weighting with innovation. The innovation in this project is not gadgets: it is making a deliberately dark building safe, quiet, comfortable and kind to the sky. Values marked **(K)** are rules of thumb from general practice; confirm exact code clauses with the guide or the NBC text before quoting them.

## 1. Lighting control

- **Zoned dimming:** every room on its own circuit and dimmer, so the light ladder can be tuned on site. A simple scene controller per zone ("open", "show", "clean", "emergency").
- **Colour temperature:** 2,700-3,000 K at low levels (warmer light feels natural when dim); 3,000-4,000 K is fine in workshops.
- **Colour rendering:** CRI 90+ for exhibits and artefacts, 80+ elsewhere.
- **Glare:** deep-recessed downlights, snoots, honeycomb louvres; no bare sources in a visitor's eye line, especially in dim rooms where pupils are wide.
- **Daylight:** sensors in the Sun Gallery dim electric light as daylight rises.
- **Night-vision friendly:** red or amber step lights below 1 lux in dark rooms; red light keeps dark adaptation better than white (K).
- **Maintenance:** fibre-optic illuminators and drivers in accessible voids, never above the seats.

## 2. Emergency and fire safety in dark rooms

The critical service issue for this concept.

- **Classification:** museum = NBC 2016 Part 4 Group D-3 (assembly); the dome with fixed seats behaves like D-2 (K for D-2 use; confirm with the guide).
- **Exit signs** stay lit at all times, including during shows; photoluminescent backup strips at floor level.
- **Emergency lighting** on battery or generator, switching on automatically; escape routes lit to a minimum level (K: around 1 lux on the floor along the route is a common emergency standard, confirm the NBC value).
- **Alarm link:** the fire alarm raises house lights to full in every dark room and stops shows automatically.
- **Exits** separate from the light locks; at least two exits from each dark room and the dome (K).
- **Travel distance and exit widths:** NBC Part 4 sets maximum travel distance and occupants per unit exit width for assembly buildings. The exact numbers could not be read online; get the NBC 2016 Part 4 tables from the college library and fill them in here (gap).
- **Materials:** non-combustible false ceilings (NBC Part 4 cl. 3.4.6.2 as noted in the codes file); fire-rated fabrics and acoustic panels; fire-retardant-treated timber.
- **Detection:** smoke detectors above and below false ceilings and in the dome void; manual call points at each exit.

## 3. HVAC and humidity

Kodaikanal is cool (mean maximums about 17-21 °C, minimums about 9-13 °C) and very humid (82% annual, 87-89% June-November). So the job is **fresh air, dehumidification and mild heating**, not cooling.

- **Fresh air** for sealed dark rooms and the dome. Rough sizing for the dome (K, using a common assembly rule of about 2.5 L/s per person plus 0.3 L/s per m²): 90 seats × 2.5 + 113 m² × 0.3 ≈ **260 L/s ≈ 930 m³/h**. Check with the services guide.
- **Low velocity, low noise:** large diffusers or displacement supply under seats; silencers on ducts. Air noise is very audible in dark, quiet rooms.
- **Dehumidification:** for exhibits, electronics, fibre illuminators and the dome surface. Stable humidity matters more than an exact number; around 45-55% RH is a common museum target (K, CCI guidance stresses stability).
- **Heating:** low-temperature radiant or heated air for winter mornings and evenings (event nights).
- **Heat recovery:** an energy-recovery ventilator saves heating energy in a cold, humid climate.
- **Plant room** (≈80 m²) away from the dome and star chamber to keep noise out.

## 4. Acoustics

- **Dark rooms rely on hearing:** in the star chamber and dome, sound replaces sight. Rooms must be quiet (low background noise from air and plant) and not echoey.
- **Dome focusing:** a hemisphere focuses sound at its centre. The cure is a **perforated dome screen with absorbent material behind it**, so sound passes through and is absorbed.
- **Reverberation, worked example (Sabine formula, K):** RT = 0.161 × V ÷ A.
  - A 12 m dome with a 1.5 m wall below the springline holds about V ≈ 620 m³.
  - For a target RT of about 0.6 s, total absorption A ≈ 0.161 × 620 ÷ 0.6 ≈ **166 m² sabins**.
  - The seats and audience, carpet and the backed perforated dome together must supply that. Show this calculation on the services sheet; reviewers like seeing a number.
- **Star chamber:** felt or PET acoustic panels on walls, carpet or rubber floor, upholstered benches.
- **Sound isolation:** between the dome and the workshop/event space (double walls or a buffer corridor), and from plant.

## 5. AV and exhibits power

- Projection room for the dome with the server rack; ventilated and quiet.
- Power and data points inside exhibit plinths, with access panels for maintenance.
- Keep technology light: mechanical and analogue exhibits (orrery, phases ring, prism) need no screens; a few screens only where they add real value (live Sun data, show previews).

## 6. Dark-sky exterior lighting

- Shielded, fully downward fittings; no light above the horizontal.
- Warm or amber light, 2,700 K or lower (K: amber/PC-amber LEDs are common in dark-sky places).
- Timers and an event curfew, so the observing terrace and KSO's sky stay dark.
- Low bollards and step lights for paths instead of poles.
- This protects the observatory next door, which is part of the design argument.

## 7. Water, drainage and building envelope (site link)

- About 1,580 mm of rain a year, wettest October-November: covered approaches, generous roof overhangs, sloped roofs or well-drained flat roofs, and waterproofing for any space sunk into the slope (dome and dark rooms), with drainage behind retaining walls.
- Rainwater harvesting is normally required for new buildings in Tamil Nadu (K; check the hill-station rules).

## 8. What to put on the services sheets

1. Lighting layout and RCP per zone with lux targets and the dimming zones.
2. Emergency lighting and exit plan, with travel paths and exit signs.
3. HVAC zoning diagram: fresh-air supply to dark rooms, plant location, quiet routes.
4. Dome acoustic section with the RT calculation.
5. Dark-sky exterior lighting plan with fitting details.
6. Accessibility route plan.
