## 1. Where the proposed site is

From Ritu's topic-review sheet (page 3): an open plot on the main road beside the **Kodaikanal Solar Observatory (KSO)** and close to the **Government Rose Garden**, outlined in red on a satellite image. Plan drawn at 1:300, roughly 66 m x 66 m outer grid.

Her site argument (keep it, it is strong):
- Completes an existing route: Rose Garden by day, observatory, then the museum, a "sky and science trail".
- Hotels can package stargazing evenings and museum passes, which helps the off-season.
- The residential neighbourhood gets a venue for talks, astronomy club nights, eclipse events.
- Audience: tourists and hotel guests, school groups, students and researchers.

**To confirm at site stage:** survey number and ownership of the plot, exact area, contours (it is a hill site; the plan must respond to slope), access road width, and whether the plot falls in any restricted zone under the current master plan.

## 2. The observatory next door (why the museum makes sense)

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| KSO founded 1 April 1899, after an 1893 committee chaired by Lord Kelvin chose Kodaikanal for its "southern, dust-free, high altitude location" | [KSO history](https://kso.iiap.res.in/new/history) (A), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodaikanal_Observatory) (B) |
| Evershed effect (radial flow of gas in sunspots) discovered here, January 1909, by John Evershed | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodaikanal_Observatory) (B) |
| More than 100 years of daily solar images (Ca II K, white light, H-alpha, prominences); around 200,000 glass plates, now digitised | [KSO archive/history](https://kso.iiap.res.in/new/history) (A), [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/experiences/heritage/an-iconic-observatory-celebrates-125-years-recording-the-sun) (B) |
| Solar tower telescope built 1960; early helioseismology | Wikipedia (B) |
| Existing museum is "mainly pictorial, supported by a few telescope models", with a live solar image and Fraunhofer spectrum shown depending on sky conditions; also an 1857 transit instrument, a coelostat explainer, "Tree Rings and Solar Cycles", Evershed effect panel. About 400 m from the main gate | [IIA museum page](https://www.iiap.res.in/centers/kso/astronomy-museum) (A), [KSO museum](https://kso.iiap.res.in/new/museum) (A) |
| Museum open daily 9:30-12:00 and 14:00-16:30; night sky-watch 6:30-7:30 pm (Outlook gives 9-4 and 7-9 pm: conflicting) | IIA museum page (A), Outlook (B) |
| Group visits need an online form at least 15 days ahead; Rs 50 per head; max 90 minutes on campus with a coordinator; only demarcated areas | [IIA visiting KSO](https://www.iiap.res.in/outreach/visit-us/visiting-kso/) (A) |

Design takeaways for the thesis:
1. **Gap the museum fills:** the real observatory is restricted, pictorial and weather-dependent. The new museum is open, hands-on, works on cloudy days, and sends interested visitors on to KSO. Position it as a partner, not a competitor.
2. **Local story to build exhibits on:** the 125-year solar record, sunspots, the Evershed effect, the coelostat, spectroheliographs. This makes the Sun Gallery uniquely "Kodaikanal" rather than generic.
3. Ritu wrote that cameras are not permitted and night viewing needs permission. The pages read did not state a camera rule. Confirm on a site visit and photograph the signage (the "restrictive access" argument is stronger with evidence).

## 3. Climate (design drivers)

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Altitude | about 2,133 m (infobox) to 2,225 m (text) | [Wikipedia Kodaikanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodaikanal) (B, conflicting) |
| Mean daily max | 16.9 °C (Nov) to 21.2 °C (May) | Wikipedia 1991-2020 table (B) |
| Mean daily min | 8.8 °C (Jan) to 13.0 °C (May); record low 2.7 °C | same |
| Rainfall | about 1,579 mm/yr; wettest Oct (270 mm), Nov (260 mm), Sep (200 mm); driest Jan | same |
| Humidity (evening) | 82% annual; 87-89% Jun-Nov | same |
| Sunshine | about 1,965 h/yr | same |

Implications (K):
- **No air-conditioning load for cooling;** the issue is warmth and damp. Plan for passive solar gain in the bright Sun Gallery, thermal mass, and heating or warm finishes in dark/sedentary rooms (dome theatre, workshop).
- **Humidity and mould:** avoid untreated MDF/particle board in exposed areas; specify moisture-resistant boards (IS 303 MR grade or BWP where needed), breathable finishes, dehumidification for exhibit cases and electronics.
- **Mist and cloud** are frequent in the wet months: this supports her argument that an indoor "sky" (dome theatre, star chamber) is needed when the real sky is hidden.
- **Dark sky value:** a hill town at over 2,000 m with low light pollution compared with plains cities. Exterior lighting should be shielded, warm (2,700 K or below) and pointed down, so the museum does not damage the observatory's sky. This is a strong "services/innovation" point.

Thesis use: with a 10 m height cap, a **dome theatre must be sunk partly below ground** or kept small. A half-sunken dome also helps with the slope, acoustics and keeping the "descent into darkness" literal: bright upper level, dark lower level. Planning rules are only 15% of marks, so a short compliance sheet is enough.

## 5. Access

- Ghat road SH-156 from NH-44 near Batlagundu (about 52 km); second route via Palani. Peak-season traffic is one-way on major roads. (Wikipedia, B)
- Nearest rail: Kodaikanal Road (80 km), Palani (64 km). Airport: Madurai (about 115-133 km). (Wikipedia, Outlook, B)
- Tourist numbers: about 3.2 million (2009, Wikipedia) and 7.1 million (2017, DT Next). Use the newer figure with its date.
- Implication: coach parking and drop-off for school groups and hotel shuttles; most visitors arrive in groups in a 2-4 hour window.
